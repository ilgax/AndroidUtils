# Utils

One Android app (`dev.ilgax.utils`, Galaxy Tab S9 FE class tablets, Android 14+) that bundles small system tools as independent modules, with a Material 3 Compose UI that adapts from phone to 12-inch tablet. Everything privileged goes through [Shizuku](https://shizuku.rikka.app/).

It installs next to the older apps, but **turn the old Speaker Kill Switch OFF and uninstall it first**: two apps pinning the speaker would fight. Likewise uninstall the standalone **Tab Remote Receiver** and re-pair the watch (keys are per app).

## Modules

| `:speaker` Speaker kill switch | While ON the speaker emits nothing, **alarms included**. (1) Blocks the built-in speaker for every audio product strategy (`setDeviceAsNonDefaultForStrategy`). (2) Zeroes **every stream** on the built-in speaker devices (`TYPE_BUILTIN_SPEAKER`, `SPEAKER_SAFE`): music, notification, ring, system, alarm, DTMF, accessibility, through the hidden `IAudioService.setDeviceVolume`/`getDeviceVolume` (or `setStreamVolume` while the speaker is the active route). Each original index is saved separately and all are restored on OFF; the ringer mode is saved and restored too (zeroing RING can flip it to vibrate). A stream whose minimum is above 0 (alarm often 1) is set to its minimum and logged. The 0.7.0 single saved index is migrated as the music value. (3) **Per-app mute:** an `AudioPlaybackCallback` (and every enforce) reads the full playback configurations through the Shizuku-wrapped `IAudioService.getActivePlaybackConfigurations`; every app with a started player routed to a built-in speaker (for example TikTok sent to the speaker by Samsung Sound Assistant's "Separate app sound", which bypasses both the routing block and the volume) gets its `PLAY_AUDIO` app op set to ignored through `IAppOpsService.setMode`. The previous mode is persisted first. **Sticky (0.8.1):** a caught app stays muted when it pauses, stops or swipes to the next video (un-muting then let the next video out loud on the speaker until the next scan); it is given back on OFF, when one of its players reports a known route that is not a speaker (an unknown route counts for nothing), or when it has had no player at all for 10 minutes (checked on scans only). ## Equation solver: supported math

Pure Kotlin, exact big-rational arithmetic; steps are (LaTeX line, short caption). Anything else fails with "Can't solve this yet, it needs X".

- **Input.** `:`, `÷`, `\div`, `\colon` divide (same precedence as `/`, left to right with `·`). `\le \leq \leqslant ≤ <=`, `\ge \geq \geqslant ≥ >=`, `<`, `>`, `\lt`, `\gt`; `\ln \log \lg \exp`, `\operatorname{log}` and `\mathrm{ln}` spellings; `\pi`, `π`, `\mathrm{e}`. `\times` stays the variable x.
- **Constants.** π and e are exact inside coefficients (`3x=2\pi` gives `x=\frac{2\pi}{3}≈2.094`). The letter `e` is Euler's number whenever the input has another letter, or is an expression with no `=`; it is a variable only when it is the only letter of an equation, or the unknown named by a trailing `e=` line. `\mathrm{e}` is always Euler's number.
- **Algebra (one unknown).** Linear, rational equations (excluded values rejected), quadratics with surd roots, chained equalities, linear systems (any size, Gauss), simplification.
- **Higher degree.** Common power of x, binomials `xⁿ=k` (exact `∛2`), even polynomials by `u=x²` (shown), rational root theorem with synthetic division until a quadratic is left, repeated roots reported as (double), (triple). What is left has its real roots found with Sturm sequences and exact bisection and is printed as decimals with the step "No exact roots; approximate" (no root is missed, none invented; works on any degree up to the exponent limit of 64).
- **Inequalities.** Linear (the sign flips for a negative divisor, said in the step caption), chained linear (`1<2x+1≤7`), polynomial of any degree and rational ones through a sign chart (zeros, denominator zeros, test points); answers like `x ∈ (-∞, -2) ∪ [3, ∞)`, `ℝ`, `∅`, a single point. Denominator zeros are always open; endpoints are exact where the roots are, decimals otherwise.
- **Radicals.** `\sqrt{expr with x}` and `\sqrt[n]{…}`: isolate, raise to the power, solve (any degree), check. Two square roots take two squarings; two same-order roots with nothing else take one. Even roots add a domain step; every candidate is checked in the original equation and extraneous ones are shown as rejected.
- **Exponentials.** `b^{f(x)}=K`: same base after rewriting when K is a perfect power (`2^{x+1}=8`, `9^x=27`, rational bases), otherwise logarithms (`3^x=5` gives `x=\log_3 5≈1.465`); `a^{f}=b^{g}` by equal bases or by taking ln; substitution `u=2^x` for `4^x-3·2^x+2=0`, rejecting u ≤ 0; `e^{x}` and `\exp`.
- **Logarithms.** `\log_b`, `\log` (base 10), `\lg`, `\ln`. Numbers evaluate exactly when they can (`\log_4 2=1/2`, `\ln e=1`), otherwise they stay symbolic with a decimal; same-base logs are combined (power, product, quotient rule) and different related bases are converted first (change of base); `\log_a f=c` becomes `f=a^c`, equal logs give equal arguments, a quadratic in `\ln x` is substituted. A domain step lists the log arguments; every candidate is checked.
- **More logarithms (0.9.2).** *Inequalities:* `\log_2(x-1)>3`, `\ln x\le1`, `\log_{1/2}x>2`, `\log_3(x^2-1)<1`, `\log x+\log(x-3)\le1`, `\ln(x+1)\ge\ln(2x-3)`; the domain is every argument > 0, logs of related bases are reduced to an algebraic inequality (the direction flips for a base below 1) that goes through the sign chart, anything else (`\ln x<x-2`, `\log_2 x>\log_3 x`) gets its boundary points from the equation solver (decimals when not exact) and one test point per interval; the answer is intersected with the domain, sampled against the original inequality, and shown as `x>9`, `x∈(0, e]`, a union, "No solution" (empty domain included), or the whole domain. `≠` is not accepted in inequalities. *Change of base:* `\log_2 3\cdot\log_3 8=3`, `\log_4 3\cdot\log_3 8=3/2`, `\log_a b\cdot\log_b c=\log_a c`, `\frac{\log 8}{\log 2}=3`, `\frac{\ln x}{\ln 2}=\log_2 x`; a rational coefficient moves into the argument when that is nicer (`\frac12\log_2 9=\log_2 3`); `\log_e` is `\ln`; `\log_2 x\cdot\log_x 8=3` is true for every `x>0, x≠1`. *Unknown base:* `\log_x 8=3` (x=2), `\log_x 16=2` (x=4, -4 rejected), `\log_x 4=-2`, `\log_x(2x+3)=2` (x=3, -1 rejected), `\log_x 1=0` (every x>0, x≠1); one such log equal to a rational number only, checked for base > 0, base ≠ 1, argument > 0. *Logs of roots, powers and logs:* `\ln\sqrt x=2`, `\log_2(4^x)=6`, `\log_3(9^x\cdot3)=5`, `\ln(e^{2x})=4`, `\log_2\sqrt{x+1}=1` (log of a root, of a power of a positive number, or of a product of those, with the rule named in a step), and `\ln(\ln x)=0`, `\log_2(\log_3 x)=1`, `\log_2(2^x+1)=3`, `\log_2(\sqrt x+1)=3` (the outer log is undone first, for a whole-number value).
- **Numeric fallback.** For one unknown mixed in a way no exact method covers (`x+2^x=5`, `\ln x=x-2`) a scan of -1000…1000 for sign changes gives decimals, labelled approximate. A root where the curve only touches zero would be missed.
- **Percentages.** `%20` (Turkish, sign first), `20\%`, `20%`, `\%20` and spaced variants become the exact fraction `(20/100)` in the normaliser (so a prefix `%` never reaches KaTeX, where it would be a comment) and the steps start with "Write %20 as 20/100". `%20x=15`, `20\% x=15` and `x+%10x=55` use ordinary implicit multiplication. Answers are plain numbers; no percent-change word problems.
- **Absolute value.** `|…|`, `\left|…\right|`, `\lvert \rvert`, `\vert`, `\mid` (`\lVert \rVert` and `\|` are read as absolute values, with a note). A bar after an operand closes an open bar, otherwise it opens one (nested `||x|-1|` and adjacent `|x||x-1|` work). Numbers are exact (sign of surds and π decided exactly). Equations: `|f|=c` (none / f=0 / f=±c), `|f|=|g|`, `|f|=g` (both signs, then checked, so `|x-1|=2x` rejects -1); anything else is split at the zeros of every bar's polynomial argument into cases with the bars replaced by ±argument, solved per interval, kept only inside it and checked (`|x-1|+|x+2|=5`, `x^2=4|x|`, nested bars). Inequalities: `|f|<c`, `≤`, `>`, `≥` (c ≤ 0 gives ∅, ℝ or f ≠ 0, a single point for `≤0`), general `|f|⋚g` by cases and sign charts. Not with roots, logs, powers of the unknown or trigonometry inside the bars, nor bars around a fraction with an unknown in a multi-bar problem.
- **Trigonometry.** `\sin \cos \tan \cot \sec \csc`, `\arcsin \arccos \arctan`, `\sin^{-1}`, `\sin^2 x`. Without brackets the argument is only the following run of juxtaposed factors (`\sin 2x+1` is sin(2x)+1, `\sin 2x\cos x` is sin(2x)·cos(x)); with brackets exactly the bracketed part. Exact values at every multiple of 15° (`\sin 15°=(√6-√2)/4`, `\tan 90°` is "undefined"), numeric to 4 decimals elsewhere, simple identities (`\sin^2 20°+\cos^2 20°=1`). **Angle unit** (setting "Angles", Auto by default): Auto = degrees if a `°` (`°`, `^\circ`, `^{\circ}`, `\degree`) appears or no π does, radians if π appears and no `°`; Degrees and Radians force it (in degrees a π inside a trig argument means 180). Answers always show the unit (`30^{\circ}`, `\frac{\pi}{6}`). Equations in one unknown with linear arguments: `f(ax+b)=c` for all six functions (|c| out of range: no solution; exact base angle for standard values, else inverse function numerically), general solution (`x = 30° + 360°k, x = 150° + 360°k, k ∈ ℤ`, in a step), solutions in one turn `[0°, 360°)` / `[0, 2π)` as the answer; polynomials in one function (`u=\sin x`, roots with |u|>1 dropped), sin and cos mixed through sin²+cos²=1 when one of them is only squared, `a\sin x=b\cos x` through tan. Every solution is checked in the original equation. Not supported (said honestly, never guessed, and the numeric search is not used): different arguments (`\sin 2x=\cos x`, no double-angle identities), different functions other than the sin/cos case, `\sin x+\cos x=1`, `\sin x\cos x=…`, the unknown outside the functions (`\sin x=x`), trigonometry in inequalities, systems, hyperbolic functions.
- **Derivatives (0.6.0).** Notation: `\frac{d}{dx}(…)`, `\frac{d}{dx} expr`, `\frac{d}{dt}` (any single letter), `\frac{dy}{dx}` after `y=…`, `y'` after `y=…` (same line or the one above), `f(x)=… \\ f'(x)`, `f'(x)=`, `f'(2)`, `f'(a)`, `f''`, `f'''`, `y''`, `\frac{d^2}{dx^2}`, `\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}`, `f^{(n)}(x)` and `(…)'`. Every prime spelling (`'`, `\prime`, `^{\prime}`, `^{\prime\prime}`, `’`, `′`) is normalised. A line `f(x)=…` (also `g`, `h`, `F`, `G`, `H`; `y=…` when `y'` or `dy/dx` is asked) defines the function; `f(x)=12` or `a(b)=12` stay products. Rules: constants, powers with any rational exponent (`\sqrt`, `\sqrt[n]{}`), constant multiple, sum, product, quotient, chain, `e^u`, `a^u`, `u^w` (logarithmic differentiation), `\ln`, `\log_b`, `\log`, `\sin \cos \tan \cot \sec \csc`, `\arcsin \arccos \arctan`, `|u|` (`u/|u|·u'`, with the note "Undefined at x = 2"). Steps name the rule (Power rule, Product rule: (uv)' = u'v + uv', Quotient rule, Chain rule …), a sum is done term by term, and the raw rule output is shown before "Simplify". **Representation:** the parser's `Node` tree is differentiated directly (it is what the parser, the numeric and exact evaluators and the printers already speak, so no new expression type); the unsimplified rule output is an intermediate step, then `Alg` turns it into a canonical sum of terms `coef·Πbase^exp` (exact surd coefficients, rational exponents: like terms collected, like powers merged, `x^{1/2}` printed as `\sqrt{x}`, negative powers as fractions, a shared denominator or `e^u` factor pulled out, `\sin^2+\cos^2=1` only when two terms match exactly), and a rational function with a real denominator goes through `Poly`/`RatFn` (expand, cancel, one fraction with a factored denominator such as `\frac{-2}{(x-1)^{2}}`; a cancelled factor is reported as "Valid for x ≠ 1"). Terms are written in descending powers; `\tan` gives `\sec^{2}x`. **Cross-check:** every derivative (the rule output, and the printed result parsed back) is compared with a five-point central finite difference at up to five random points of the domain (step h and h/10; points near poles, kinks or fast oscillation are skipped); a mismatch makes the card say it is unsupported rather than show a wrong answer, and a function with no real values to test on gets a note. **Angles:** calculus is in radians, so with the setting on Auto a derivative problem is read in radians unless a `°` is written; Degrees forces degrees (every derivative of a trigonometric function then gets a factor π/180, with a note), Radians forces radians. **Values:** `f'(2)` is exact where the numbers are (`\frac{\sqrt3}{2}≈0.866`, `2\pi≈6.2832`, `e`), else a decimal; `f'(a)` substitutes. **Applications** (only from the notation): `f(x)=…` alone or with `f'(x)` gives f' as the answer and, when f' is a polynomial or rational function, steps for "Critical points: solve f'(x)=0" (the existing solver), the sign chart of f' (the existing inequality code) with the intervals of increase and decrease, and local maxima and minima with their exact f values; `f(x)=… \\ x=a` (also `x_0=a` and `\text{tangent at } x=a`) gives the tangent line `y=mx+b`; `f'(x)=0`, `f'(x)=4` or `y'=0` solve that equation with whichever solver fits (polynomial, rational, trigonometric, exponential …). Not supported (said honestly): implicit and parametric differentiation, partial derivatives, the limit definition, integrals, piecewise functions, a derivative inside a derivative, hyperbolic functions.
- **Limits (0.7.0).** Notation: `\lim_{x\to a}`, `\lim\limits_{x \to a}`, `\to`, `\rightarrow`, `\longrightarrow`, `->`, `→`, `\operatorname{lim}`, bare `lim`, subscripts with or without braces, any single-letter variable, `a^{+}` / `a^{-}` / `a^+` / `0+` for one side, `\to\infty`, `\to -\infty`, `\to +\infty`, and a trailing `=` is ignored. **Methods, tried in this order and shown as named steps:** (1) direct substitution, done by an extended-real evaluator that knows infinities, the sign of a vanishing denominator on each side, bounded `\sin`/`\cos` of something that runs off (so `\frac{\sin x}{x}` → 0 as x → ∞ and `x\sin\frac1x` → 0 as a squeeze) and names the indeterminate form (0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, ∞−∞, 1^∞, 0^0, ∞^0); (2) 0/0 of a rational function: `(x−a)` is divided out of numerator and denominator with the polynomial engine, cancelled, and the result is looked at again, as often as needed; (3) the conjugate for one square root (or two) in a sum, in either part of the quotient or in an ∞−∞ difference, with the cancelled fraction substituted; (4) x → ±∞ for rational functions (divide by the highest power; equal degrees give the ratio of leading coefficients, a lower numerator degree gives 0, a higher one ±∞ with the sign worked out for x → −∞) and for algebraic expressions with roots (`\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x}` → ±1; x = −t is substituted so every root is read at its highest power); (5) standard limits, matched through equivalent infinitesimals of a polynomial argument u with u(a)=0 (`\sin u`, `\tan u`, `\arcsin u`, `\arctan u` ~ u, `1−\cos u` ~ u²/2, `e^u−1` ~ u, `\ln(1+u)` ~ u, also inside products, quotients and integer powers, so `\frac{\sin 3x}{x}` → 3, `\frac{\sin 5x}{\sin 3x}` → 5/3) and `(1+g)^h` with g → 0, h → ∞ as e^{lim g·h} (`(1+\frac{k}{x})^x` → e^k, `(1+x)^{1/x}` → e); trigonometric limits are in radians always (a note says so); (6) L'Hôpital's rule for 0/0 and ∞/∞ (up to 3 rounds; numerator and denominator are differentiated separately with the existing `Differentiator`, `Alg` and `DerivCheck`; the steps are named "L'Hôpital: 0/0 form (round k)" and show the derivatives; while rounds remain the standard-limit table is not used again, so `\frac{x-\sin x}{x^3}` shows its three rounds), after rewriting `0·∞` as a quotient (the logarithm, inverse trigonometric or polynomial factor goes on top), `∞−∞` of fractions as one fraction, and the indeterminate powers through `u^w=e^{w\ln u}` only when the exponent's limit is found. **One-sided and non-existent:** a pole gets its sign from the sign of the denominator on each side (existing numeric sign sampling, exact where the denominator is a polynomial); when the sides differ the answer is "Does not exist" with both one-sided values in the steps (`\frac1x` at 0, `\frac{|x|}{x}` at 0, where |u| is replaced by ±u for the side); a two-sided limit of a function defined on one side only (`\sqrt{x}` at 0) uses that side, with a note; `\sin\frac1x` at 0 and `\sin x` at ∞ are "Does not exist (oscillates)" (only when the whole expression is `\sin`/`\cos` of something that runs off, and checked by sampling). **Values** are exact (fractions, surds, π, e: `e^{2}≈7.389`) plus a decimal. **Safety check (mandatory):** before any finite or infinite answer is shown, the function is evaluated at a ± 10^-2 … 10^-10 (or x = ±10^3 … 10^12) in 70-digit `BigDecimal` when the expression is rational, algebraic or has roots and |x|, otherwise in doubles at a ± 10^-2 … 10^-6 and x up to 10^6 (small steps are included because e^x overflows); the values must converge to the claimed one (finite), keep the claimed sign and grow without bound (±∞), or really oscillate. On disagreement the card says "Can't solve this limit reliably" and shows no answer. **Other letters:** `\lim_{h\to0}\frac{(x+h)^2-x^2}{h}` (the limit definition of a derivative of a polynomial) is expanded, h is factored out and cancelled (→ 2x). Not supported (said honestly): multivariable limits, sequences with n!, sums or products, limits that need a series or more than three L'Hôpital rounds (`\frac{\sin x-x}{x^5}`, `\frac{2^x}{3^x}`), a stated answer after `=`, an unspecified function `f(x+h)`, ε-δ proofs, and oscillating expressions other than a bare `\sin`/`\cos`.
- **Not supported:** nested radicals; `x^x` (unknown in base and exponent); inequalities containing roots, powers of the unknown or trigonometry, chains such as `1<\ln x<2`, and inequalities with an unknown base; an unknown base next to other logarithms or terms (`\log_x 8+\log_2 x=4`), or a cancelling chain with the unknown next to other terms (`\log_2 x\cdot\log_x 8+x=4`); a nested log equal to a non-integer; systems with roots, logs, powers, absolute values or trigonometry; non-linear systems; complex roots; division by a sum of surds.

## Install and first run

1. Install `dist/utils-0.9.2-debug.apk` (arm64 only) (signed with the shared debug key, so later builds update in place).
2. Start Shizuku (wireless debugging) and open Utils; use the banner to grant Shizuku permission. Allow notifications when asked.
3. Quick Settings: edit tiles and add **Speaker kill**, **Hotspot** and **Solve**. Long-press the launcher icon for the **Speaker switch** and **Hotspot** shortcuts.
4. Watch remote: open the module and work through Setup (Bluetooth permissions, notifications, Bluetooth on, accessibility, battery exemption). For a sideloaded app, allow restricted settings first: App info, three-dot menu. Then "Pair a watch", compare the 6-digit code with the watch, confirm on the tablet, then on the watch.

5. Equation solver: open the module and finish Setup (enable the accessibility service "Utils", or Shizuku plus "Display over other apps"). **Upgrading from 0.2.0:** the two old accessibility services ("Watch remote" and "Utils – screen capture for Solve") are replaced by one shared service named "Utils"; turn it on once in Settings → Accessibility (sideloaded: App info → three-dot menu → "Allow restricted settings" first, Android 13+). Then add it to the S Pen menu: Air command → Settings → Shortcuts → add **Solve**. Try the solver with no model in the "Try it" field.

## Adding a module

1. New Android library `:foo` depending on `:core` only; implement `UtilModule` (card state, `toggle()`, `Detail`).
2. Declare its tile/service/activities in its own manifest.
3. `include(":foo")` in `settings.gradle.kts`, `implementation(project(":foo"))` in `:app`.
4. Add one line to `ModuleRegistry.of(...)` in `UtilsApplication`. Home card, detail pane and dynamic shortcut follow automatically.

## Watch app

`wear/` is the Wear OS app "Tab Remote" (`dev.ilgax.utils.wear`): BLE central, app and tile that send signed commands to the Utils tablet side (`modules/remote`, wire format in `modules/protocol`, pure Kotlin/JVM with JUnit tests). Build it with `./gradlew :wear:assembleDebug :wear:lintDebug`; `:remote` bundles the resulting APK, and at the 0.5.0 release the release build for the one-time manual install is `dist/utils-wear-0.5.0.apk`. Its debug APK is signed with `keystore/watch-debug.keystore` (certificate SHA-256 `97ce8589...1dbd8`).

**Watch 0.5.0 / Utils 0.9.0 (new package name, clean break).** The watch app is now `dev.ilgax.utils.wear` (was `dev.ilgax.tabremote.wear`); the label stays "Tab Remote". Android treats it as a different app, so it cannot update the old one and has no pairing key. The wire protocol is unchanged (the shared module's package is now `dev.ilgax.utils.protocol`; UUIDs, frames and the HMAC and pairing labels are as before), and so is the signing key. Pairing a new watch replaces the stored pairing on the tablet and forgets the old watch's version, update offer and verify result. **Migrating (once):**
1. Install the new watch app by hand (there is no APK in `dist/` any more, the watch updates in-app: build it with `./gradlew :wear:assembleRelease`, or take the 0.5.0 watch APK from the repository history at commit `7dc2c06`, `dist/utils-wear-0.5.0.apk`): `adb install <that apk>`, then `adb shell appops set dev.ilgax.utils.wear REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES allow`.
2. Uninstall the old "Tab Remote" (`dev.ilgax.tabremote.wear`): `adb uninstall dev.ilgax.tabremote.wear`.
3. Pair again: Utils > Watch remote > Pair a watch, then Pair here on the watch (this replaces the old pairing).
4. Re-add the Tab Remote tile on the watch.

**Watch 0.4.2 / Utils 0.8.4 (efficiency, no protocol change).** BLE scan is balanced-mode with a hardware filter on the service and the tablet's manufacturer data (id, or the pairing flag), stops on the first match and gives up after 6 s; a tablet seen in the last 10 min is connected to directly (2.5 s) before any scan. The link is reused between commands and dropped 5 s after the last one (kept while the app is visible); HELLO, MTU, service discovery and the notification subscription happen once per connection; a user's tap jumps ahead of queued housekeeping jobs; the connection is opened as soon as the app opens or the tile comes into view. The visible offer poll is 30 s while the subscribed link is up (the tablet's ping does the work), 5 s without it, backs off to 60 s on failures and stops with the screen. Bluetooth turning off fails the running job at once. The watch UI updates its views in place instead of rebuilding them, debug builds run StrictMode (log only), the key and preferences are read off the main thread, and Guava and the explicit protolayout dependency are gone (APK 8.4 MB to 5.7 MB). The watch APK Utils embeds is now the R8-shrunk `release` build (same signing key, not debuggable, English only, dex deflated): 5.7 MB to ~0.2 MB. Pure policies (idle, direct-connect expiry, poll cadence, scan filter) are in `modules/protocol` `LinkPolicy.kt` with tests. **Only a device can confirm:** the real tap-to-command latency, that the combined service and manufacturer-data scan filter matches on the Watch8's controller, that a direct connect to a remembered address works and falls back cleanly after the tablet's address rotates, and the battery effect.

**Listener notification (Utils 0.8.3).** The "Listening for watch" foreground notification now lives on a minimum-importance channel (`remote_min`; the old `remote` channel is deleted): collapsed at the bottom of the shade, silent, no status-bar icon, no badge. **Watch updates (Watch 0.4.1, Utils 0.8.2).** Utils > Watch remote > Watch app > Update starts a one-shot HTTP server (random token path, first client address only, stops after one full download; 10 min, pushed to at least 5 min after the watch first asks so retries find it) and offers it over BLE (GET_OFFER, then an HMAC-authenticated long read of the OFFER characteristic bound to that frame's nonce). The watch must be on the tablet's hotspot or the same Wi-Fi.
- **Picking up the offer.** App open: the watch asks for an offer every 5 s while it is visible and paired (15 s after a failed check), and also reacts to the tablet's BLE ping when it holds a subscribed connection. The ping alone was not enough: the watch only subscribed inside an offer check, so after any BLE link drop nothing re-connected it while the app stayed open. A fresh offer (under 3 min) starts by itself; an older one shows "Update to vX". App closed: after any successful command (tile, quick launch) the watch reads the offer and posts a "Tab Remote update ready" notification; tapping it opens Tab Remote and starts the update. The notification needs the watch's notification permission (asked once).
- **Bluetooth first, Wi-Fi as the fallback (Watch 0.5.1, Utils 0.9.1).** The APK (about 211 KB) goes over the authenticated GATT link; no L2CAP. New characteristic UPDATE_DATA `f6863211-e975-4981-af7b-aab8996e9d34` (write + notify). The watch reads a NONCE, writes `token(16) | offset(4) | window(2) | tag(16)` (tag = HMAC-SHA256 over "upd", the nonce and the body, 16 bytes; the token is the live offer's) and the tablet answers with notifications `offset(4) | total(4) | data`, sized to the negotiated MTU minus 3 (ATT) and 8 (header): 236 data bytes at MTU 247, 506 at 517 (the watch now asks for 517; the link settles on the smaller side; it needs at least 41). Stop-and-wait: windows of up to 16 KB, the watch asks for the next when the previous is complete; a lost notification shows up as an offset gap and the watch re-requests from its last good byte. Chunks carry no MAC on purpose: the watch checks the full SHA-256 against the HMAC-authenticated offer and the signing certificate against the running app before installing, so a forged or corrupted stream can only fail the transfer. Offer flags bit 3 = Bluetooth available (same format and length; old watches ignore it and keep using HTTP, and an old tablet never sets it so a new watch uses HTTP). The HTTP URL and token stay in the offer as the fallback. The tablet serves from the embedded asset, only for the live offer's token (cancel, expiry, a new token or "Wi-Fi only" end it), one window and one watch at a time (`0x9D` gone, `0x9E` busy; others queue between notifications). Utils > Watch app > **Update over**: Bluetooth (default) / Wi-Fi only. The watch holds CONNECTION_PRIORITY_HIGH during the transfer and restores balanced after, appends to a temp file and resumes from its length after a drop. It falls back to Wi-Fi, and says so on screen, when the offer lacks the flag, the characteristic or MTU is missing, the tablet refuses, 3 windows fail in a row, there is no progress for 20 s, or the finished file fails its hash. Expected transfer time (an estimate, not measured): about 5 to 15 s at either MTU (917 vs 428 notifications; throughput is set by the connection interval and data length, so MTU 517 only saves a few percent of header overhead).
- **Download.** Wi-Fi network request per attempt (released on every path), up to 3 attempts with 2 s then 6 s backoff, the SHA-256 from the offer is checked, and the APK's signing certificate must equal the running app's (`getPackageArchiveInfo`, GET_SIGNING_CERTIFICATES) or nothing is installed. A spent token (HTTP 404, or a download that completed but hashed wrong) makes the watch report RETRYING and ask for a fresh offer; the tablet then restarts the one-shot server with a new token, inside the original 10 min window. Progress and failures (no Wi-Fi, download, checksum, wrong key, install) go to the tablet with UPDATE_STATE; the tablet shows them, a timeout message, and a **Retry** button. A second offer while an update runs is ignored, and an offer that already ran is not restarted by polling.
- **Install.** Stale PackageInstaller sessions from earlier attempts are abandoned first. The session sets `USER_ACTION_NOT_REQUIRED`, and on API 34+ `setRequestUpdateOwnership(true)`; the manifest holds `UPDATE_PACKAGES_WITHOUT_USER_ACTION` (without it the system ignores the flag), `ENFORCE_UPDATE_OWNERSHIP` and `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`. **Platform rule:** a silent update only works when Tab Remote is already the installer of record, so the first self-update after an `adb install` still asks once on the watch; after that updates are silent. When the system does ask, the watch launches the confirmation at once if the app is visible, or posts a notification that opens it, and says it should be the last time. The watch's version line shows the installer of record and update owner (`getInstallSourceInfo`).
- **After the update.** The process restarts; on the next launch the watch sends HELLO with the new version and the installed-binary report, and Utils shows "Updated to x".
- **Installed-binary check.** The watch hashes its own installed APK file(s) (streamed, off the main thread; base then splits, one SHA-256) and its signing certificate, and writes `format(1) | versionCode(4) | apkSha256(32) | certSha256(32) | tag(16)` to the new VERIFY characteristic `4a7d93c0-18e5-4b2f-a6c9-0d5e7f31b862` (tag = HMAC-SHA256 over "vfy", the NONCE read just before, and the body; needs the negotiated MTU). It sends it on the first launch of every new version, when the tablet's "Verify watch app" sets the request flag in the next GET_OFFER (bit 2), and from the watch's own "Verify watch app" button. Utils compares it with the embedded APK (`assets/watch-update.properties` carries `versionCode`, `versionName`, `apkSha256` and `certSha256` of the watch key) and shows "Watch app verified (0.4.1)" or one of: corrupted (same version and key, other bytes), different build (other version), wrong signing key. A mismatch offers **Reinstall**, which offers the embedded APK again with the force flag (OFFER flags bit 1) even at the same version.

Pairing: on the tablet tap "Pair a watch" (2 minute window), on the watch tap Pair; both show a 6-digit code. Compare, then Confirm on the tablet first, then on the watch. A new pairing replaces the old one (one watch only).

Protocol notes:
- Commands: PING 0, LOCK 1, PLAY_PAUSE 2, VOLUME_UP/DOWN 3/4, RING 5, KILL_APP 6, OPEN_APP 7 (slot 0..3), SYNC_APPS 8, SWITCH_APP 9 (slot 0..3), HELLO 10 (versionCode), GET_OFFER 11, UPDATE_STATE 12, RUN_MACRO 13 (3-byte macro id), SYNC_MACROS 14 (page index), SPEAKER 15 (one arg byte: 0 query, 1 on, 2 off, 3 toggle; the reply is the state in the response status, see below). Old peers answer 0x06, which is handled.
- Result codes: 0 success, 0x05 auth failed, 0x06 unknown command, 0x90 failed, 0x91 accessibility off, 0x92 pairing rejected / not active, 0x93 rate limited, 0x94 nothing to close, 0x95 slot empty, 0x96 could not start app, 0x97 MACRO_BUSY, 0x98 MACRO_NOT_FOUND, and the SPEAKER replies 0x99 kill switch off, 0x9A on, 0x9B off and 0x9C on while Shizuku is not ready on the tablet (a requested change was not made), and for UPDATE_DATA 0x9D offer gone / not Bluetooth, 0x9E another transfer running. A tablet whose Utils predates SPEAKER answers 0x06 and the watch says "Update Utils on the tablet". They avoid Android's local stack statuses 0x80..0x8F (such as 133).
- OFFER characteristic `9c4e7a12-3b58-4f6d-8e21-6a0d5b7c9f34`, MACROS characteristic `e1c2a7d4-5b39-4a86-bd10-72f3c8a94e55` (read + notify; the notification is a content-free ping, first byte 1 = update offer, 2 = macros changed). OFFER flags: bit 0 offer present, bit 1 force, bit 2 verification requested, bit 3 Bluetooth transfer available. UPDATE_STATE phases 1 downloading, 2 verifying, 3 installing, 4 failed, 5 retrying (value = attempt); failures 0 unknown, 1 no Wi-Fi, 2 download, 3 checksum, 4 install blocked, 5 install failed, 6 wrong signing key.
- Macro list pages: `format(1) | version(4) | total(4) | page(2) | n(1) | n * (id(3) | nameLen(1) | name UTF-8) | hasMore(1)`, 32 byte tag, at most 480 bytes; the watch pages until `hasMore` is 0.

Security model:
- Pairing: ephemeral ECDH P-256; both devices derive a 6-digit code from the shared secret and the user compares them (numeric comparison). The tablet keeps the new key only if the user confirmed there and a valid PING signed with it arrives within the pairing window.
- Every command is `cmdId | args | tag`; tag is 16 bytes of HMAC-SHA256 over a fresh 16-byte per-connection nonce plus the command. The nonce is replaced after every attempt, so a captured frame cannot be replayed. Verification is constant time; 5 consecutive failures lock a device out for 30 s.
- The long-term key lives in the Android Keystore on both devices (non-exportable HMAC key), never on disk or in logs.
- Not protected: the BLE link itself is not encrypted (commands are authenticated, not confidential), and the advertisement exposes a random tablet id. The watch tile activity is exported so the tile host can start it; it only accepts a fixed set of commands.

Troubleshooting: "Tablet not found" means check the service is running, Bluetooth is on, battery optimisation is off for Utils and the watch is close. "Enable accessibility on tablet": turn on the "Utils" accessibility service (allow restricted settings first for a sideloaded app). "Not authorised, re-pair": the tablet's key was removed or replaced; pair again.
